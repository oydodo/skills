# Crux-First Reasoning

**Goal:** Find what currently governs the user's problem and help them understand *why*. Identify the dominant tension before prioritizing uncertain assumptions; an unanswered question is not automatically the main issue.

## Method

1. **Anchor the goal and gap.** What does the user actually need to achieve, decide, or understand? Contrast that with the observed state. Do not confuse a requested checklist, a proposed method, or completed activities with the underlying outcome. Reframe only if the original framing materially misleads.
2. **Identify the dominant tension.** Trace the relationships and constraints producing the gap; distinguish symptoms, drivers, binding constraints, and secondary factors. Compare plausible diagnoses. Ask which relationship, if changed, would most alter the outcome *at this stage*. Ground the choice in evidence, not in what is most visible, most often mentioned, or least certain. Multiple interacting drivers or external conditions may dominate; do not force one cause.
3. **Qualify the diagnosis, then find key uncertainties.** Explain why the leading diagnosis matters more than alternatives, and what evidence supports or challenges it. Mark an underdetermined diagnosis as provisional. **Only after identifying the likely dominant issue**, find unknowns that could change that diagnosis or the response. Prioritize verification by **consequence if wrong × weakness of evidence**, using the cheapest discriminating check. If the dominant issue is already established, do not manufacture uncertainty or delay action.
4. **Explain the decisive reasoning to the user.** Organize findings around the main issue, not the order of source material or tool output. Connect the key observations to the causal or constraining relationship, show why secondary factors do not currently drive the decision, and state the judgment, practical implication, and conditions that would change it. Present a concise, checkable argument that helps the user apply the approach elsewhere; do not dump every intermediate thought or force a fixed report template.

## Boundaries

- Treat "dominant" as relative to the goal, stage, and evidence, not as an immutable single root cause. A lack of decisive evidence is a reason for a discriminating test, not a license to invent a major contradiction.
- Maintain evidence discipline without requiring `independent-judgment` to be loaded; do not duplicate its generic critique checklist.
- Match depth to stakes. Routine inventories and straightforward execution need their requested result, not a philosophical diagnosis or unasked-for lesson. If explanation does not improve understanding or action, keep it brief.
- Fulfill the user's requested output (answer, plan, code, or document). Writing Skills may polish expression but must not replace the underlying judgment.
